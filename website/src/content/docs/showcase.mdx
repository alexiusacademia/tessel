Tessel is made for real desktop apps, and the best proof is apps people use.
Everything on this page is written in Tessel from the first line to the last:
the interface, the calculations, the file formats, the printing. Each one is a
single native program of a few megabytes.

## Tally

A spreadsheet. Tally opens and saves Excel and OpenDocument workbooks and
calculates what Excel calculates: over 490 functions, dynamic arrays, `LET` and
`LAMBDA`, charts, tables, pivot tables, a solver, and printing with a live
preview. An edit recalculates only what depends on it.

About 70,000 lines of Tessel. The grid, the formula engine and the `.xlsx`
reader and writer, zip and encryption included, are all Tessel code.

## Quill

A word processor. Quill shows a document as pages, the way it will print, with
styles, lists, tables, pictures, headers and footers, footnotes, a table of
contents, comments, tracked changes and spelling as you type. It opens and
saves Word, OpenDocument and Rich Text files, and exports PDF.

About 50,000 lines of Tessel. Breaking paragraphs into lines and pages, and
drawing them, is done on a [`Canvas`](/ui/drawing/); a keystroke in a 300-page
document takes a few milliseconds.

<LinkButton href="https://quill.syncster.dev/" icon="external">quill.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Slope

An engineering app for the stability of slopes. Slope finds the factor of
safety two independent ways, by finite elements with strength reduction and by
slip circles, with seepage, rapid drawdown, loads, earthquakes, and nails,
anchors and geogrids. It writes the report as a PDF.

The mesh generator, the finite element solver and the plots are Tessel code:
the language is fast enough for numerical work, not only for buttons and lists.

## What they have in common

- **One language, top to bottom.** No web view, no second language for the
  hard parts, and no UI toolkit underneath: Tessel draws the interface itself.
- **Small.** Each of the three apps above is a download of under 5 MB.
- **Tested without a window.** Each has scripts that drive it headless and
  compare what it prints, as described in [Testing apps](/ui/testing/).
- **Everything from the standard library.** Tally and Quill update themselves
  in the background, with the network, files and processes of the
  [standard library](/reference/stdlib/).
