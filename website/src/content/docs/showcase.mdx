Tessel is made for real desktop apps, and the best proof is apps people use.
Everything on this page is written in Tessel from the first line to the last:
the interface, the calculations, the file formats, the printing. Each one is a
single native program of a few megabytes.

## Tally

A spreadsheet. Tally opens and saves Excel and OpenDocument workbooks and
calculates what Excel calculates: over 490 functions, dynamic arrays, `LET` and
`LAMBDA`, charts, tables, pivot tables, a solver, and printing with a live
preview. An edit recalculates only what depends on it.

About 70,000 lines of Tessel. The grid, the formula engine and the `.xlsx`
reader and writer, zip and encryption included, are all Tessel code.

<LinkButton href="https://tally.syncster.dev/" icon="external">tally.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

</section>

## Quill

A word processor. Quill shows a document as pages, the way it will print, with
styles, lists, tables, pictures, headers and footers, footnotes, a table of
contents, comments, tracked changes and spelling as you type. It opens and
saves Word, OpenDocument and Rich Text files, and exports PDF.

About 50,000 lines of Tessel. Breaking paragraphs into lines and pages, and
drawing them, is done on a [`Canvas`](/ui/drawing/); a keystroke in a 300-page
document takes a few milliseconds.

<LinkButton href="https://quill.syncster.dev/" icon="external">quill.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Keystone

A project scheduler with Microsoft Project's scope: tasks and outlines, all
four kinds of link with lags, calendars, the critical path, resources with
leveling, costs, baselines, progress and earned value, and the Gantt chart,
network diagram, resource, usage, calendar, timeline, S-curve and report
views. Type a plan as an indented outline, drag bars to move and link tasks
while the critical path follows, and branch the plan to compare what-ifs.

About 73,000 lines of Tessel. The scheduler, the leveling, and the readers and
writers for Microsoft Project XML, Primavera P6 XER and Excel are Tessel code;
it schedules 2,000 tasks in about 10 ms.

<LinkButton href="https://keystone.syncster.dev/" icon="external">keystone.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Reel

A media player for nearly every video and audio format, with no FFmpeg inside: every
container and every decoder is written in Tessel. H.264, HEVC, AV1, VP9 and
VP8, MPEG-2, VC-1 and ProRes; AAC, Opus, AC-3, DTS-HD Master Audio and Dolby
TrueHD; MKV, MP4, transport streams, DVD and Blu-ray folders. On top of that, a
library of films, shows and music, find in dialogue, seek previews, an
equalizer, picture settings, network streams and lossless clip export.

About 170,000 lines of Tessel, checked picture by picture against the codecs'
conformance streams: all 146 HEVC and all 242 AV1 test streams decode
bit-exact. The heavy decoders run as parse and reconstruct stages in
background jobs, so a 1080p HEVC picture takes about 20 ms. Reel is open
source.

## MathPad

A math notebook in the spirit of Mathcad. Math is typed the way it's
written, as fractions, roots, integrals and matrices, on real pages worked out
from the top down, and every answer can show its steps. Numbers carry units;
algebra and calculus are done in symbols, from equations and systems to
derivatives, integrals and differential equations; with matrices, numerical
methods, small programs, 2D and 3D plots, tables, sliders, and export to PDF
and LaTeX.

About 90,000 lines of Tessel. The computer algebra with its step-by-step
working, the math typesetting and the plots are Tessel code, drawn on a
[`Canvas`](/ui/drawing/).

<LinkButton href="https://mathpad.syncster.dev/" icon="external">mathpad.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Slope

An engineering app for the stability of slopes. Slope finds the factor of
safety two independent ways, by finite elements with strength reduction and by
slip circles, with seepage, rapid drawdown, loads, earthquakes, and nails,
anchors and geogrids. It writes the report as a PDF.

The mesh generator, the finite element solver and the plots are Tessel code:
the language is fast enough for numerical work, not only for buttons and lists.

<LinkButton href="https://slope.syncster.dev/" icon="external">slope.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Flume

An engineering app for open channel flow. Flume works out normal and critical
depth, the hydraulic jump and water surface profiles as you type, for
rectangular, trapezoidal, circular, parabolic and surveyed sections, draws the
channel as it goes, and writes the calculation up as a PDF report.

<LinkButton href="https://flume.syncster.dev/" icon="external">flume.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Forebay

An engineering app for reservoir operation studies. From a record of the
flows into a reservoir, Forebay works out period by period what it could have
supplied to water supply, environmental flow, irrigation and hydropower, where
its level would have stood and what it would have spilled, with rule curves
and a flood control level. It finds the firm yield and yield against storage,
searches for rule curves, makes synthetic inflow records, and routes floods
through the spillway.

The operation study, the rule curve search (differential evolution over
many runs of the study) and the synthetic records are Tessel code; the long
ones run on a background thread, with their progress shown, while the window
stays responsive.

<LinkButton href="https://forebay.syncster.dev/" icon="external">forebay.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## Girder

An engineering app for the analysis and design of structures. Girder models
frames in three dimensions, analyses them under static and seismic loads, with
P-Δ, natural modes, the response spectrum method and time history, and
designs steel and concrete members, slabs and footings by NSCP 2015.

The stiffness solver, the eigenvalue solver and the three-dimensional view are
Tessel code.

<LinkButton href="https://girder.syncster.dev/" icon="external">girder.syncster.dev</LinkButton>

## What they have in common

- **One language, top to bottom.** No web view, no second language for the
  hard parts, and no UI toolkit underneath: Tessel draws the interface itself.
- **Small.** Tally, Quill and Slope are each a download of under 5 MB.
- **Tested without a window.** Each has scripts that drive it headless and
  compare what it prints, as described in [Testing apps](/ui/testing/).
- **Everything from the standard library.** Tally and Quill update themselves
  in the background, with the network, files and processes of the
  [standard library](/reference/stdlib/).
